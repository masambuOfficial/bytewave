Here's a list ordered roughly by impact per effort. The first four groups are free.

Free

1. Technical and on-page SEO (I can do most of this in the codebase)
- Give every page a unique <title> and meta description, plus canonical URLs.
- Add Open Graph and Twitter card tags so links look good when shared.
- Add JSON-LD structured data. Organization and LocalBusiness for the home page, Article for blog posts, Service and Product for those pages, and FAQPage for /faqs.
- Check that each page has one <h1>, a sensible heading order and alt text on images.
- Compress images, use WebP, lazy-load them, and check Core Web Vitals in Search Console. Speed is a ranking factor.
- Make sure www and non-www, and http and https, all redirect to a single version.
- Add noindex to pages you don't want ranked, such as admin, auth and unsubscribe pages.
- Improve sitemap.xml. Priorities are all 0.7 and the static pages have no lastmod.

2. Search Console follow-up
- Use URL inspection → Request indexing on your key pages (home, services, products and portfolio).
- Watch the Pages report for "Crawled, not indexed" and other errors.
- Use Performance to see which queries you actually get impressions for, then improve those pages.
- Set up Bing Webmaster Tools. You can import your Search Console property in one click. Bing also feeds DuckDuckGo and Yahoo, and partly feeds AI search tools.

3. Google Business Profile
- Fill in every field: categories, services, hours, service area, description and website link.
- Upload real photos regularly, and post updates.
- Ask every happy client for a Google review, and reply to all reviews. Reviews are one of the biggest local ranking factors.
- Keep your business name, address and phone number identical everywhere.

4. Content and off-page
- Write blog posts and case studies that answer what clients actually search for. Examples are "SACCO management system Uganda", "school management software" and "website design company in [your city]". Your auto-fetched news posts are generic tech news and won't help much. Original, expert content will.
- Turn portfolio items into detailed case studies with results and client quotes.
- Add local directory listings, such as Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, local business directories, Clutch, and LinkedIn and Facebook company pages. Keep the details consistent.
- Get backlinks from client sites (a "Built by ByteWave" footer link), local associations and guest posts.
- Share content on social media. It doesn't rank directly, but it helps discovery.
- Add internal links between related services, products and blog posts.

Paid (for later)

- Google Ads (search campaigns) for immediate visibility on high-intent keywords.
- Google Local Services or local ads if they're available in your region.
- SEO tools: Semrush, Ahrefs or Moz for keyword research, competitor gaps and backlink tracking. Screaming Frog has a free tier of up to 500 URLs.
- Paid backlinks and sponsored posts on reputable local and industry sites. Avoid link farms, which can get you penalised.
- Content writers or an SEO agency for a steady stream of quality content.
- Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn ads to bring traffic while organic rankings build.
- Review and reputation tools, and a paid directory such as Clutch's sponsored listings.

Ranking takes time. Expect 3 to 6 months of consistent work before results show for competitive keywords. Your brand name should rank much sooner.
